Chapter 05 / Through the Pandemic

My busiest month
was the Netherlands'
hardest month.

Fourteen months on a bike. Also fourteen months of a pandemic. Lockdowns, a curfew, closed restaurants — the same conditions that emptied Dutch streets filled my delivery queue.

01 / The Permit in My Pocket

While the country
stayed inside,
riders were the ones
still on the street.

On 23 January 2021, the Netherlands imposed its first curfew since the Second World War. 21:00 to 4:30, nobody outside without a reason. Delivery riders were one of the exceptions.

03 / The Reopening Crash

The day the Netherlands
reopened, my income
fell by more than half.

April 2021 was my single busiest month in 14 months of delivery — the tail end of curfew, restaurants still closed for dine-in. Six weeks later, the government's "openingsplan" reopened restaurants on 5 June. The very next month, my trip count collapsed.

Restaurants reopened their dining rooms on 5 June 2021. Customers who had been ordering delivery for six months went back to eating out. My income didn't dip — it fell off a cliff.

04 / What the Pandemic Actually Was, Economically

The pandemic didn't interrupt my work. The pandemic was the demand.

Every restriction that emptied a restaurant filled my queue. Every curfew that cleared the streets left riders as the only visible workers. When the country reopened, the platform I depended on lost more than half its purpose overnight.

I didn't choose this job because of the pandemic. But the pandemic decided how much that job was worth, month by month, without ever asking me.

That's the kind of relationship a spreadsheet can show you, and a memory alone cannot.
