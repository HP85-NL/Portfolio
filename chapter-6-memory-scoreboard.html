Claim 01 / 05
Busted

Thursday was not the secret good day.

Memory

Thursday — Thursday felt like the secret good day nobody talked about. — Field log

Data

Thursday had the lowest per-trip earnings of any weekday — €5.58, below every other day. Friday paid €6.07, Saturday €5.98, even Monday paid slightly better.

The Thursday feeling probably came from tipping, not earnings — midweek customers tipped at ~12%, weekend customers at ~10%. You remembered warmth, not money.

Claim 02 / 05
Partially Busted

The 8-hour shift almost never happened.

Memory

I have calculated sometimes that I work 8 hrs and I make around 100 in an 8hrs shift. — Field log

Data

In 14 months, exactly 2 sessions lasted 6 hours or more. My median session was 41 minutes. The "8-hour shift" was a memory of clock time — hours logged in — not active delivery time.

Active hourly rate was €16.86 median, well above the €12.50/hr the €100/8hr claim implied. The number I remembered underestimated my real hourly.

Claim 03 / 05
Confirmed

The 4km trip really did pay less per km.

Memory

I often feel that I ride 4km or more and that delivery often ends up paying less compared to delivering the food within 3 or 3.5 kms. — Field log

Data

Per-km efficiency collapsed from €7.24 to €2.13 — a 3.4× drop between shortest and longest trips.

Structural reason: pickup (~€2.40) and dropoff (€1.00) are flat regardless of distance. That €3.40 gets diluted the further you ride. Uber's fare engine rewards pickups, not kilometers. My body knew before I did the math.

Claim 04 / 05
Reframed

Surge was mealtimes, not weather.

Memory

UBER often provides in that extreme weather like 1.3x, 1.4x or even 2.0x that encourage the riders to deliver. — Field log

Data

Surge fired at lunch (47%) and late dinner (48%). At 10 AM — 81 trips, 0% surge. The weather doesn't stop at 10 AM. The clock does.

Surge was Uber's demand-management tool, not their thank-you for riding in the rain. The +34% bonus was real. The reason wasn't what I thought.

Claim 05 / 05
Confirmed

Winter really did tip better.

Memory

During the cold days, it is very difficult to ride the bike. Sometimes, customers appreciate it and they compensate with tip. — Field log

Data

February 2021 — the coldest, darkest, hardest-lockdown month — peaked at 14.6% tip rate. Summer 2021 dropped to 8-9%.

Customers had no idea how far I rode or whether surge was firing. What they saw was the weather outside their window. Tips followed the season, not the effort.

The lesson

Memory tells stories. Data tells patterns. Neither one alone is the truth.

Every claim I tested came from a body that had lived on a bike for 14 months. That body was often right — the 4km felt wrong for a reason, the winter tips were real. But it was also sometimes wrong — Thursday was ordinary, surge had nothing to do with the sky.

The point isn't which side wins. The point is that only by putting them next to each other do you learn anything at all. That's what a data analyst does.

That's why the last 14 months on a bike also became the first chapter of my career.
