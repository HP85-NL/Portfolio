Chapter 07 / Why This Made Me a Data Analyst

The question
followed me
off the bike.

"How does the app decide?" I couldn't answer that question while I was riding. But I never stopped asking it. Eventually, that question became a career.

01 / What Six Chapters Actually Proved

I wasn't just
delivering food.

Chapter 1 introduced three lenses I didn't have words for at the time. By the end of this project, all three had become real, tested skills.

01 · Product
Reading a system
I reverse-engineered how Uber priced trips, triggered surge, and structured incentives — without ever seeing their internal logic.
02 · Consumer
Reading a market
I noticed how Dutch customers tipped, ordered, and behaved differently by season, city, and day of week — a market I mapped by hand.
03 · Data
Reading the evidence
A year later, I tested every instinct against 23,486 rows of my own GDPR data. Some intuitions held. Some didn't. Both were useful.
What connects all three isn't the bike, or Uber, or the Netherlands. It's the habit of noticing a pattern, forming a hypothesis, and going to find out if it's actually true.
02 / Naming What I Already Had

The MBA wasn't
the start.
It was the formalization.

Why Data Analytics

The MBA was the need of the hour. I chose a specialization in Data Analytics because I knew it would give me the confidence to bridge complex data and data insights into business decisions — combining a technical foundation with a business one.

What I Learned at Work

The people who manage stakeholders and bridge the gap between data complexity and business language are the ones with the steeper growth curve in any company. Technical skill alone isn't the differentiator. Translation is.

My current title is Sales Analyst, Commercial Insight. That word — commercial — is the job. Not just running the numbers, but making sure the numbers change what the business actually decides to do next.

04 / The Last Line

I didn't set out to become a data analyst. I noticed a pattern, and I couldn't stop asking why.

That's the whole job, really. Not the tools, not the charts, not the dashboards. Just the refusal to accept "that's just how it works" as a full answer.

Fourteen months on a bike gave me the question. This project is the answer I finally went looking for.
