04 / The Fee I Never Noticed

The biggest single
line item wasn't distance.

€8,100
Pickup fees
43% of total earnings

Pickup was flat €2.40 per trip. Uber paid it the moment I accepted an order — before I moved a pedal.

Distance paid €6,326. Dropoff paid €3,213. Surge paid €2,351. But the largest single component of what Uber paid me was the flat fee for *showing up*, not for the kilometers I actually rode.

This is why the 4km trip felt worse than the 3km trip. The distance fee grew slowly. The €3.40 fixed base — pickup plus dropoff — was the same either way.

05 / What Uber Took (and Didn't)

The commission logic
had one loophole.

Uber took a commission on base fare — pickup + distance + dropoff. But two categories were classified as "non-commissionable" — surge and tips. Every euro from those went straight into my pocket, untouched.

06 / What I Never Saw

On the bike, I saw one number per trip. In the ledger, every trip was a small structured decision Uber had made about how to value the work.

Ten categories. Three types of income. Two hidden bonuses. One commission structure. All of it running quietly under the "€6.75" that flashed on my screen when the delivery closed.

This is what a data analyst does — read the shape of a system through the way it counts money.
